## What This Does

<!-- The path a submission takes, from arriving to the label a reader sees.
     Six or seven steps — this is the trace you wrote in Milestone 1. -->

1. **Step 1** - The /submit endpoint receives the request or writing which is a JSON with text and creator_id.

2. **Step 2** - Validity check happens here so that if text is missing, empty, not text, too short or too long, it rejects the request here by sending back an error rather than letting bad input reach the signals. If validity passes, the text gets a content_id. The content_id adds the identity that /appeal will use later to find the item. A missing creator_id is also rejected, and every rejection is logged.

3. **Step 3** - The model / meaning signal (detector.py) measures how predictable the text is to a language model and adds a model_score (a number from 0-to-1).

4. **Step 4** - The style signal (stylometry.py) measures the text as text by looking at the sentence-length spread, word variety and punctuation, and adds a style_score (a number from 0-to-1 for how AI-like the style looks, higher means more AI-like).

5. **Step 5** - The combiner (scoring.py::combine_signals) merges the two numbers thereby adding combined_score. It applies my weights and adds a single score. The confidence is calculated in the route (how far the score is from 0.5).

6. **Step 6** - This is the label mapping (scoring.py::score_to_label) step where the result is mapped to one of three written variants (high-confidence AI, high-confidence human, and unsure). It adds the plain-language message a non-technical reader will see, and the score ranges must leave no gaps, so every reachable score lands in exactly one variant.

7. **Step 7** - At this stage, the JSON carries content_id, model_score, style_score, the guess, the confidence, and the label text. The record is appended and saved as a permanent record to logs/audit.jsonl, then it sends the answer back to the writer. A later appeal will have something to point at since it is added as a new entry beside the original, which stays untouched.

## Signals and Scoring

<!-- For each signal: what property it measures, why that might differ between
     human and AI writing, and WHAT IT CAN'T SEE.

     The blind spot is not optional. If you can't name it you don't understand
     the signal yet — and unit 8's attack set is built out of blind spots. -->

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:** Signal one measures how easily a language model can guess each next word in the text, almost like how autocomplete works.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** Since AI is trained on data and over time it learns to associate words or patterns together, it picks the most likely next words that are easy to guess. A human has the autonomy to utilize life experiences to make choices.

**What it can't see:** Novice writers with simple, predictable writing may be flagged as AI. Unfamiliar wording generated by AI can also be flagged as human.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:** It measures the variability of sentence length, the variability of vocabulary and the amount of punctuation used.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** AI writing will have more even rhythm due to the model following a familiar format. A person's writing is more of an uneven rhythm due to varying patterns of writing.

**What it can't see:** It can't read what the text says, so a person can intentionally add typos or misplaced or cut-up sentences, to pass AI text as human. My test proved this as lightly changed AI text scored as human (0.32).

### The combining rule

<!-- Write the rule in words, then give the numbers. What happens when the two
     signals disagree? -->

**The rule:** My rule takes a weighted average of model_score and style_score but trusts model_score more, because errors like typos or cut-up sentences can be intentionally introduced to make AI generated text label as human. When the two signals disagree strongly, the service can only be "unsure", never "AI", because wrongly accusing a real writer is very upsetting and hurtful for the writer.

**The numbers:**

- Weight for model signal = 0.6
- Weight for style signal = 0.4
- Strongly disagree means there is a gap between the two scores greater than 0.4, and then the combined score can't exceed 0.6
- Label = human when the combined score is less than or equal to 0.35, Label = AI when combined score is greater than or equal to 0.65, Label = unsure when combined score is greater than 0.35 and less than 0.65

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

<!-- ⚠️ The grader checks your code against that line. If your rule lives
     somewhere else, say where. -->

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:**

When I submitted a paragraph I wrote myself, the model signal gave 0.41 but the style signal gave 0.68. My rule combined the scores into 0.52, which is in the "unsure" range. The gap between the scores is 0.27 which is under 0.4, so there is no need for the cap. The text does not approach AI range, so a real writer is not labelled AI.

## Label Variants

<!-- The exact text of all three labels, as a reader would see them, and the
     score range each covers.

     Write them for someone who has never heard the word "threshold". -->

| Label | Score range | The exact text a reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| high-confidence human | 0.00 – 0.35 | This piece sounds more like it was written by a real human being. |
| unsure | above 0.35, below 0.65 | This piece is hard to decipher whether it was written by a real human being or whether it was AI-generated. This is not an accusation; lots of writing by real human beings falls here. You are free to ask for someone to take a look and provide an opinion. |
| high-confidence AI | 0.65 – 1.00 | This piece looks like it has been AI-generated to us. If you disagree, you can appeal and a person will review it. |

**Why I worded the "unsure" one this way:**
<!-- It's the hardest of the three. It has to admit uncertainty without
     sounding like an accusation, because the person reading it may well have
     written every word themselves. -->

I added "This is not an accusation; lots of writing by real human beings falls here", so that the writer understands they are not being judged and they are not being singled out. I also said "You are free to ask for someone to take a look", because they should also get a human opinion.
